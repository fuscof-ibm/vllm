# Microbenchmark results — `postprocess_vectorize_tiled`

Branches compared:

- **BASE** — `last_precompiled` (pre-vectorization baseline)
- **VEC** — `postprocess_vectorize` (uint64 loads/stores; PR #48110)
- **TILED** — `postprocess_vectorize_tiled` (this PR: adds inner-dim tiling on top of VEC)

Each request corresponds to the full per-request copy volume (conv + temporal states) for the model. Gain / Speedup columns are relative to BASE.

## Cross-platform summary

- **BASE** is capped at ~33% of HBM peak on GB200 and ~62% on H100 — largely 1-byte load/store bound.
- **VEC** (uint64 loads/stores) closes most of the H100 gap (~84–86% at `reqs≥16`) but only reaches ~65–74% on GB200: at large batch counts the extra SM/HBM ratio on Blackwell exposes launch and scheduling overhead that a single-dim grid can't hide.
- **TILED** adds a third grid dimension over the inner dim, which flattens the plateau: **~80–83% of peak on GB200 from `reqs≥16`**, **~85–86% on H100 from `reqs≥8`**. The biggest wins are at low `reqs` (1–8), where the absolute-time saving per call is 0.4–2.8 ms — the regime where this kernel is on the end-to-end critical path.
